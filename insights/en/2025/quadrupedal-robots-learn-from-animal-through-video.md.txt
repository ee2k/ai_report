Researchers from the University of Hong Kong and The Chinese University of Hong Kong have published a study in *npj Robotics* introducing an innovative framework that enables quadruped robots to learn highly agile animal locomotion skills using only **monocular videos**. The key contribution of the work is its ability to overcome two longstanding challenges: the complexity of reward engineering in reinforcement learning and the high cost of collecting 3D motion-capture data. By combining robust 2D pose estimation and tracking, a spatiotemporal graph convolutional network (STGNet) for accurate 3D pose reconstruction, and generative imitation learning, the framework efficiently transfers animal movement skills to robots. This dramatically reduces the cost of acquiring training data and allows robots to learn complex behaviors—including galloping, tripod locomotion, bipedal walking, and backflips—from ordinary videos, representing a significant advance for legged robotics and embodied AI.

**Background and Challenges**

Although deep reinforcement learning has significantly improved the locomotion capabilities of quadruped robots, their agility still falls well short of that of real animals. One major reason is that learning highly dynamic movements with reinforcement learning typically requires carefully designed reward functions, which are time-consuming to tune and often difficult to optimize successfully.

Imitation learning offers an alternative by allowing robots to learn from demonstrations, but it traditionally depends on expensive 3D motion-capture systems to obtain high-quality reference data. Such datasets are costly to collect and often limited in both scale and diversity. Learning directly from monocular videos is far more economical, but introduces significant challenges, including the loss of depth information, motion blur, and unreliable joint tracking—particularly during fast and explosive animal movements.

**The Proposed Framework**

To address these challenges, the researchers developed a three-stage learning framework:

1. **Robust 2D Pose Estimation and Tracking:** To improve pose estimation under high-speed motion and partial occlusion, the framework first applies a fine-tuned DeepLabCut model for 2D pose estimation. A skeletal graph model and Kalman filtering are then used to predict and refine joint positions at the pixel level, effectively removing outliers and generating smooth, continuous 2D joint trajectories that serve as reliable input for 3D reconstruction.

2. **3D Pose Estimation and Reference Motion Generation:** The framework employs a **Spatiotemporal Graph Convolutional Network (STGNet)**, which combines graph convolutional networks with dilated temporal convolutional networks. Graph convolutions aggregate spatial pose information, while dilated temporal convolutions capture motion dynamics over time, enabling accurate reconstruction of 3D joint trajectories from monocular videos. STGNet separately regresses local joint positions and root joint trajectories, effectively decoupling body translation from pose estimation.

3. **Generative Imitation Learning:** The reconstructed 3D motion is mapped into the robot's joint space and used as reference data for **Adversarial Motion Priors (AMP)**, a generative imitation learning algorithm. By jointly optimizing style rewards and task rewards, the robot learns to reproduce complex movements while simultaneously following omnidirectional velocity commands and respecting physical constraints such as joint torque limits.

**Experimental Results**

The proposed framework was successfully deployed and validated on the **AlienGo** quadruped robot. Experimental results demonstrated that the robot could learn and perform a variety of challenging locomotion skills, including:

- **Galloping:** Achieved a maximum speed of **3.45 m/s**, demonstrating excellent agility during high-speed locomotion.
- **Tripod Gait:** Successfully maintained stable locomotion using a three-legged gait, showing fault-tolerant behavior even under simulated single-leg failure conditions.
- **Bipedal Walking:** Learned stable two-legged walking, a particularly difficult task for a quadruped robot.
- **Backflips:** Successfully performed continuous explosive backflip maneuvers.

Additional analyses of 2D pose tracking, 3D pose reconstruction, and imitation performance confirmed that the proposed framework produces smoother 2D trajectories, more accurate 3D pose estimates, and reliable execution of commanded movements.

**Discussion and Applications**

By leveraging the vast amount of publicly available monocular video data on the internet, this framework substantially lowers both the cost and technical barriers associated with teaching quadruped robots complex locomotion skills. It eliminates the need for expensive motion-capture systems and greatly reduces dependence on manually engineered reward functions.

The approach has broad implications not only for quadruped robots but also for humanoid robotics and the broader field of embodied AI. Beyond robotics, the underlying techniques could be applied to animal behavior research through automated motion analysis, neuroscience studies involving user-defined body-part tracking, and animation systems for generating realistic movements of digital characters and virtual animals.

Although the framework still has limitations—including challenges in scaling across species with significantly different body structures and occasional failures in 3D motion reconstruction—it demonstrates considerable promise as a scalable and cost-effective approach for learning agile robot behaviors directly from everyday videos.
